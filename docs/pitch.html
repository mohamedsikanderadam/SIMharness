SIMharness

Is the agent on the phone telling the truth?

Voice agents answer customers about refunds, cancellations and prices. They state those policies confidently — and get them wrong, because the policy lives on a website that changed and the agent's knowledge didn't.

Nobody finds out until a customer acts on it and demands the refund they were promised.

For the team that deployed the agent. We call it like a customer, check every factual claim against your live website, and tell you which ones it gets wrong — before a customer does.

Why live data

The thing we check against is exactly the thing that changes

A cached fact sheet doesn't catch a stale agent. It just becomes a second stale copy — and then we confidently report false claims that aren't false.

Mid-conversation, yes. The fetch is triggered by what the agent said. The caller doesn't know which policy to check until it hears a claim, so there is nothing to prefetch. Edit the page mid-call and the next claim is checked against the new wording.

What it does on its own

It compares values, and it escalates rather than accuses

  • Parses each claim into money, a clock time, a duration or a count — so "twenty pounds" and "£20.00" are one answer, and a rephrased lie is still caught.
  • Decides when it lacks ground truth and fires the lookup itself.
  • Three outcomes, not two: matches / contradicts / can't be checked. A hedge moves nothing.
  • A contradiction quotes the source and asks again. Only a repeat is recorded — one mistake isn't a lie.

ElevenLabs

Streaming TTS eleven_turbo_v2_5, raw pcm_24000 straight to the speaker — no file round-trip. Scribe realtime STT over websocket for the two-way call.

Voice as characterisation

The caller is calm and unhurried — an auditor, not an interrogator; pressure produces defensiveness, not truth. The agent under test is smooth and confident, which is the point: it is most dangerous when it sounds most certain.

Novelty, and what we'd fix next

Live web data usually informs an agent. We use it to prosecute one.

The same scrape becomes evidence in an adversarial conversation, fetched reactively, mid-call, in response to something the agent just said — then quoted back to it.

Where it doesn't work yet

  • The agent under test is simulated and we inject its wrong belief. The ground truth and the comparison are real; the target isn't yet one we don't control.
  • Only claims with a number in them. It could not check "we don't have our own parking" — and reported that as unverifiable rather than guessing.
